# Tier 0 — fix before `python -m models.train`

These two publish wrong numbers into `metrics.json`, and from there into the README, the first
time you retrain.

### 1. The capacity-fair table will contain a policy that raised zero alerts, counted as a beaten rival

`models/cost_matrix.py:1189` · `models/train.py:1926` · `models/report.py:874`, `:952`

`threshold_for_alert_budget` does `affordable = curve[curve["alert_rate"] <= alerts_per_1000/1000]`
over a sweep whose first row is a synthetic flag-nothing cut at `alert_rate == 0.0`. That row is
always affordable, so `affordable` is never empty, the `if affordable.empty:  # budget below even
one alert` branch at `:1190` is dead, and when the strictest *non-empty* cut already exceeds the
budget the function returns the flag-nothing row as an ordinary `ThresholdReport` with no marker.

**Measured** on the shipped validation split, at the shipped 20 alerts/1,000 budget: the shipped
one-line rule is `cycle_participation >= 0.0524`, and 126 of 2,947 validation accounts are tied at
`cycle_participation == 1.0`. That is 42.76 alerts/1,000 — more than twice the cap. So no non-empty
cut fits, and the rule's row comes back `tp=0, fp=0, precision=None`, cost ₹2,72,00,000, which is
exactly the cost of deploying nothing.

`models/train.py:1926` then stamps `"feasible_under_budget": True` as a hardcoded literal for
every row. `models/report.py:952-956` filters rivals only on a non-null `val_threshold` and a
non-null `test_total_cost`, so that row survives into the comparison, and the hardcoded sentence at
`:874` — "The one-line rule's apparent win on cost alone was bought with a queue the cap does not
permit, not with better detection" — is printed about a row that bought no queue at all. The header
sentence promising "re-selected on validation to fit the same budget" is false for it.

Correct behaviour: detect that the loosest affordable row raises zero alerts while the budget is
positive, and report the policy as infeasible/not-comparable, exactly as `flag everything` is
already excluded via `feasible_under_budget is False`. Compute that flag rather than writing `True`.

### 2. A hardcoded sentence saying "Measured on shipped v3 test data" is written into `metrics.json` every run, and its numbers are already wrong

`models/train.py:1129-1143`

`archetype_breakdown` writes a fixed string into `test.recall_breakdown.ring_attribution`:
`"Measured on shipped v3 test data: 4 of 119 positives are in two rings, 2 of them across
archetypes, and 4 of 24 rings show one fewer member than the generator built."` Nothing in the
function computes any of it — its own docstring concedes it "receives the feature table and never
sees the edge file".

**Measured**: `data/processed/test_features.csv` has **124** positives, not 119. The shipped
`dataset.test.n_positives` is 124 and `recall_breakdown.n_mule_accounts` is 124, sitting directly
beside the string claiming 119. The same stale 119 appears in `bootstrap_auc_ci`'s docstring.

This is the one thing in the repo that violates your own rule about numbers never being
hand-typed — and it does it *inside the artifact the rule protects*. Either compute the overlap
from the edge file and publish it as a measured field, or drop the numerals and keep the
qualitative rule.

# Tier 2 — serving path

### 4. The frozen partition is built on the projection the extractor was rewritten to replace

`api/main.py:515` · `tests/conftest.py:144` · `data/extractor.py:242`, `:269`, `:903`

Startup does `compute_louvain_communities(build_graph(ctx).to_undirected())`, while
`compute_node_features` uses `UG = undirected_projection(G)`. The extractor documents the mismatch
as open and names both offending call sites verbatim: *"⚠ TRAIN/SERVE SKEW, NOT YET CLOSED:
`api/main.py` builds its own reference projection with `build_graph(ctx).to_undirected()`, and
`tests/conftest.py`…"*. So this is not a fixed-and-documented bug; it is a documented live one.

`networkx.to_undirected()` resolves a reciprocal pair by letting one direction's attribute dict
overwrite the other's, and `best_partition` optimises *weighted* modularity. **Measured** on
`serving_context_edges.csv`: 1,092 of 18,426 undirected pairs are reciprocal and they carry 11.1%
of all transaction weight, so `to_undirected()` discards about 5.7% of the graph's total weight.
The partition handed to `extend_partition` is therefore an optimum of a graph nothing else uses,
and `extend_partition`'s heaviest-neighbour tie-break then runs on the summed graph — two weight
definitions in one path.

`tests/conftest.py:144` reproduces the same wrong call, which is why `tests/test_contract.py:428`'s
parity test cannot see it. Fix is one line at `main.py:515` plus the fixture.

### 5. The submitted batch's dates silently decide how much of *other* accounts' history survives

`api/main.py:616-631`

`window_end = max(submitted.max, context.max)`, `window_start = window_end - window_days`, and only
`context` is trimmed. A batch dated after the context end slides the window forward and drops the
oldest context edges — for accounts with nothing to do with the batch. **Measured** against the
shipped context (2025-03-02 → 2025-04-30, 60,379 edges, trained window 59.996 d), with a
4-transaction batch dated *n* days past the context end:

| slide | context edges kept | dropped | `observed_days` | `comparable` | `<50%` warning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 d | 60,379 | 0 | 59.984 | True | no |
| 1 d | 59,375 | 1,004 | 59.994 | True | no |
| 10 d | 50,685 | 9,694 | 59.994 | True | no |
| 20 d | 40,550 | **19,829** | 59.993 | True | **no** |
| 30 d | 30,154 | 30,225 | 59.994 | True | yes |

A third of the history disappears with `window_comparable: true` and no warning at all, because
`observed_days` is measured on the *merged* frame — sliding the window forward keeps it pinned at
~60 days by construction, so the comparability check is structurally blind to this. The 50% floor
does not trip until roughly 30 days.

This is the same class of defect as the per-request Louvain repartition this file documents having
fixed ("AN ACCOUNT'S SCORE DEPENDED ON WHO ELSE WAS IN THE BATCH"), and it lands on the features
ranked #1 and #4 by mean |SHAP|. Correct behaviour: anchor `window_end` to the reference graph the
partition was frozen on, and warn whenever `len(kept) < len(context)` at all.

### 6. The serving SHAP identity is checked on the probability scale; `train.py` already checks it correctly on the margin

`api/main.py:719-728` (`SHAP_IDENTITY_TOLERANCE = 1e-4` at `:184`) vs `models/train.py:2147-2168`

`train.py` gets this right — `margin_from_shap = contribs.sum(axis=1) + bias` compared against
`margin(model, X_test)` with a 1e-4 **log-odds** tolerance, and it explicitly notes 1e-4 is "three
below the 0.1 the previous check tolerated at the saturated end". `api/main.py` still runs the
older form: `reconstructed = sigmoid(clip(contribs.sum(1) + bias, -60, 60))` compared against
probabilities at 1e-4 **probability**.

Because sigmoid saturates, that converts to a wildly varying margin tolerance. At a true margin of
9 (p = 0.99988) a reconstruction error of about +1.66 log-odds passes; beyond ±40 the clip makes it
exactly vacuous. For scale, the whole HIGH→CRITICAL band is 0.371 wide in margin space at the
shipped threshold — so an undetected 1.66 log-odds error is over four times a tier band, on
precisely the CRITICAL accounts an analyst works first. The fix is to lift `train.py`'s check
verbatim.

### 7. The identity cannot detect the bias-column misindex its docstring claims to catch

`api/main.py:704-709` · `models/explain.py:126`

The docstring says the check catches "mistaking the trailing bias column for a feature, which
shifts every attribution by one". It cannot: `contribs.sum(1) + bias` equals `raw.sum(1)` for
*any* split of the `n_features+1` columns, so `raw[:, :-1] / raw[:, -1]` (correct, and what ships)
and `raw[:, 1:] / raw[:, 0]` (the off-by-one) give bit-identical totals. Under the misindex every
score stays right and every factor *name* is shifted, which is the worst possible failure for an
analyst-facing explanation.

`explain.py` guards column order and column count, neither of which distinguishes the two ends. A
check that does: for a binary booster with a scalar `base_score` and no per-row `base_margin`,
`raw[:, -1]` is identical for every row, so `np.ptp(bias) == 0` fails immediately under the
misindex. One line, and it closes the second of the two failure modes the guard was written for.

### 8. `merge_with_context` never de-duplicates, so a replayed context transaction is counted twice

`api/main.py:632`

`merged = pd.concat([kept, submitted], ignore_index=True)` with no de-duplication, and `build_graph`
then aggregates `weight=("amount","size")` and `total_amount=("amount","sum")` over the result. A
transaction present in both the request and the context file contributes twice to `weight`,
`total_amount`, `amounts` and `timestamps_ns`, inflating `in_amount_sum`, `out_amount_sum`,
`repeat_ratio`, `txn_velocity` and `burst_ratio` for both endpoints.

Not exotic: the 422 at `:812` tells the caller to "Date the transactions inside the context
window", the context file ships in the repo, and copying rows out of it is the obvious way to build
a scoreable request. Drop exact matches on `(sender, receiver, amount, timestamp)` and report the
count in the diagnostics.

### 9. The window-comparability warning names a feature that does not scale and omits one that does

`api/main.py:684-688` · `models/features.py` rule 3

The warning says "in_amount_sum, out_amount_sum and txn_velocity scale with window length".
**Measured** on the shipped context, full 60 days against the last 30, over accounts present in
both: `in_amount_sum` 2.00×, `out_amount_sum` 2.00× (correct), `in_degree`/`out_degree` 1.11×, and
`repeat_ratio` **1.84×** — nearly linear in window length, and named nowhere.

`txn_velocity` divides by the node's *own* active span (`data/extractor.py:751-763`), so it is a
rate and does not scale; on this data it came out flat-to-slightly-down. `features.py` rule 3 has
the identical gap, calling `in_amount_sum`/`out_amount_sum` "the two deliberate exceptions" when
`repeat_ratio = n_txns / n_counterparties` is a third. Name `repeat_ratio` in both places, drop
`txn_velocity`.

# Tier 3 — data generation and features

### 10. `MAX_CYCLE_LEN` — the strongest feature's only hyperparameter — was chosen by maximising AUC on the test split

`data/extractor.py:131-137`

The live comment states the procedure outright: *"Chosen empirically: on the test split,
single-feature AUC for cycle_participation rises 0.55 → 0.58 → 0.73 → 0.80 → 0.87 as the bound goes
3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 8"*, then `MAX_CYCLE_LEN = 8`.

`models/features.py` forbids exactly this: *"Feature-level decisions from here on are made on
VALIDATION… Reading it on test and then acting on it consumes the one evaluation the split exists
for."* And **measured**, `cycle_participation` is the strongest of the 18 features by test AUC
(0.868), i.e. the 0.87 endpoint of that very sweep. So `train.py`'s discipline is intact inside
`train.py` and defeated upstream: the feature *construction* was fitted to test, and every headline
number in `metrics.json` is computed on features built with that constant.

The same comment already contains a sound a-priori justification — "Ring sizes in the generator top
out at 8" — which makes the test ladder both unnecessary and disqualifying. Delete the ladder, and
if a sweep is wanted, run it on validation. This is the finding a leakage-conscious judge is most
likely to land on, because the code says it in plain English.

### 11. Account retirement makes graph density fall monotonically across three windows the guards certify as "equal"

`data/generator.py:1651` (`retire_hijacked_accounts`), `:1744` (`assert_equal_window_lengths`)

The calendar arithmetic is right — **measured** 59.996 / 59.984 / 59.993 days, no edge outside its
window, none on a boundary. But `retired = edge_cut.notna() & (organic_df["timestamp"] > edge_cut)`
deletes post-window organic traffic for every hijacked ring member, and the effect is cumulative.
**Measured** organic edges excluding camouflage/ring/fan-in: 63,481 / 58,608 / 54,607 — val is
92.3% and test 86.0% of train; nodes 3,096 / 2,947 / 2,866.

The consequence is a monotone covariate shift on the negative class in exactly the count and sum
columns. **Measured** on negatives only, train → val → test:

| feature | train | val | test | test/train |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cycle_participation` | 0.1494 | 0.1380 | 0.0932 | **0.62** |
| `clustering_coefficient` | 0.02286 | 0.02298 | 0.01862 | 0.81 |
| `out_amount_sum` | 382,887 | 350,241 | 343,653 | 0.90 |
| `in_amount_sum` | 364,561 | 346,025 | 331,040 | 0.91 |
| `in_degree` | 6.808 | 6.599 | 6.233 | 0.92 |
| `txn_velocity` | 0.03129 | 0.02966 | 0.02862 | 0.91 |

The top feature's negative-class mean drops 38% between the split the threshold is chosen on and
the split it is reported on. `assert_equal_window_lengths` and `assert_window_grid` cannot see any
of it, because they check calendar duration only — while `features.py` calls this comparability
"not a style preference, it is a correctness requirement". Note the direction: test negatives look
*cleaner* than train negatives, so the reported test precision flatters relative to data with
train-like density.

Fix: make retirement volume-neutral (reassign a retired account's standing relationships to a
replacement of the same role, or retire a matched control set in every window), and assert on the
*emitted* per-window edge and node counts, not just on duration.

### 12. `pagerank` is graph-size dependent, which is the class of feature `features.py` rule 3 rejects by name

`data/extractor.py:231`

PageRank is normalised to sum to 1, so its mean is exactly 1/N. **Measured** to nine decimals:
0.000322997 / 0.000339328 / 0.000348918 against 1/3096, 1/2947, 1/2866 — identical. That is an 8%
shift in the mean of the #2 feature by test AUC (0.786) driven purely by node count, which finding
11 is itself driving.

`features.py` rule 3 retired `community_size` for precisely this ("a raw count that grows with the
graph"), and the `cycle_participation` docstring requires features "independent of graph size, so
it transfers between the training graph and the serving graph". At serve time `merge_with_context`
adds the batch to a 2,947-node graph, so every scored account's pagerank moves by ~N/(N+k) — about
1.7% for a 50-account batch, the same order as the `community_internal_ratio` instability that
justified freezing the partition.

Emit `pagerank * N` (or pagerank relative to the uniform 1/N baseline). It is scale-free, preserves
the ordering the trees use, and costs one multiplication. *Decision-level impact unverified by
measurement — I cannot retrain here.*

### 13. Multiplicative jitter is implemented as an additive Gaussian on the multiplier, producing a point mass at the ₹10 clamp

`data/generator.py:602-606`

`value = rel.base_amount * (1.0 + random.gauss(0.0, rel.amount_jitter))` then
`round(max(10.0, value), 2)`. For `organic_merchant` (`amount_jitter = 0.55`),
P(N(0, 0.55) < −1) = 3.5%. **Measured** on the train split: 380 of 10,518 `organic_merchant` edges
(3.61%) and 164 of 9,025 `organic_frequent` edges (1.82%) sit at exactly ₹10.00 — 593 edges in
total across four organic roles. That is a hard spike at the floor, not a tail, and it inflates
`amount_cv` for the accounts holding those relationships while producing ₹10 grocery and pharmacy
payments.

Draw the multiplier lognormally — `base * exp(gauss(-σ²/2, σ))` — which is positive by construction
and preserves the intended mean.

### 14. One organic emitter draws its hour uniformly, tripling the organic night baseline the repo's own watermark analysis is measured against

`data/generator.py:682`

Every other emitter uses `_daytime_hour()` or a fixed plausible hour; the subscription mandate uses
`hour = random.randrange(24)`. **Measured** across all three splits: subscriptions are 19,567 of
178,410 organic edges (11.0%), and 24.34% of them land in 00:00–05:59 (uniform is 25%). Organic
night share is 3.69% *with* subscriptions and **1.15%** without; ring edges are 6.62%.

`_diurnal_in_span`'s docstring builds its residual argument on "organic 3.69%" versus "ring 6.62%"
and concludes the residual is 2.6×. Against the uncontaminated organic baseline it is 5.8× on ring
overall — so the fix that docstring describes is understated by its own contaminated baseline, and
the claim elsewhere that all organic traffic is diurnal is false for 11% of it. Draw the
subscription hour from `_daytime_hour()` once per mandate (keeping it fixed per relationship, as
now), then restate the residual.

### 15. Camouflage partners are never consumed from the pool, so one negative account bridges up to five rings

`data/generator.py:1414`

`other = random.choice(hijack_pool)` picks without removing, and `_emit_camouflage` is called with
the whole remaining pool for every `fresh_mule`. **Measured**: train has 131 camouflage partners, of
which 54 (41%) are counterparties of mules from two or more distinct rings, up to five. `_seat_ring`
was rewritten to eliminate exactly this coupling for feeders, arguing "a grouping key that repeats
an entity across groups does not group". Camouflage sharing is the identical coupling one hop out,
and those edges carry weight 2–6 so they do enter the repeated-edge cycle core. Consume camouflage
partners from the pool as the feeder sampler does. *Effect on CV folds unverified — negatives all
carry `ring_id = -1`, so no GroupKFold group is split directly.*

# Making the model logically stronger

Five changes, in order of ratio of rigour gained to hours spent. All are inside your existing
architecture — no new dependencies.

**Make every feature scale-free.** This is the single biggest logical strengthening available, and
it is nearly free. Publish `pagerank * N` instead of `pagerank` (finding 12), and express
`repeat_ratio` and the two amount sums per observed day. Right now three of your features encode
the size and duration of the graph they were measured in, which is why findings 5, 9, 11 and 12 are
all really the same finding wearing four hats: *a level compared against a threshold calibrated on
a different span.* Once every feature is a rate or a ratio, the window-slide bug stops mattering,
the retirement density decay stops mattering, batch size stops mattering, and `features.py` rule 3
becomes true rather than aspirational. You had already declined rate-normalisation as
over-engineering; I think that was the wrong call, and findings 11 and 12 are the measured reason.

**Move `MAX_CYCLE_LEN` to validation** (finding 10). One comment and one sweep. It converts your
strongest claim from attackable to clean.

**Fix the retirement volume asymmetry** (finding 11), and add an assertion on emitted edge and node
counts, not just calendar days. Your equal-window guarantee is currently enforced on the one axis
that was never the problem.

**Lift `train.py`'s margin check into `api/main.py`** and add `np.ptp(bias) == 0` (findings 6, 7).
You already wrote the correct version; the serving path just hasn't caught up.

**Anchor the serving window to the reference graph** (finding 5) and de-duplicate the concat
(finding 8). Together these make a score a function of the account and the reference graph alone —
which is the property you already argued for when you froze the partition, applied consistently.

# Real-time readiness, honestly scoped

You told me to leave the over-engineered infrastructure out, and I still think that was right —
so let me split this by what the 2026-09-05 deadline can actually absorb.

**Achievable this week, and each one is a real answer to "is this production-ready?"**

Anchoring the window and de-duplicating (findings 5, 8) is what makes the service *idempotent* —
the same account scored in two different batches gets the same score. That is a stronger and more
demonstrable claim than any streaming diagram, and it is two small edits. Publishing
`break_even_probability` in `ScoringResponse` lets a caller re-derive every tier boundary from the
response alone, which is what makes an alert auditable after the fact; right now
`threshold_used` gives them three of four. Rounding the threshold to the same six decimals as the
score removes a ~4e-7 band where a genuinely-above-threshold account is tiered down
(`api/responder.py:360`) — the error direction is alert *suppression*, which is the one direction a
fraud system should never fail in silently. And measure your latency: a table of p50/p99 for
`/score` at batch sizes 1, 10, 100 is worth more to a judge than an architecture slide, because it
is the thing nobody else in the room will have.

**The honest wall, and worth saying out loud in the writeup rather than papering over.**

Your latency floor is `compute_pagerank` and `compute_cycle_participation` over a ~60k-edge merged
graph, recomputed per request. That is the real obstacle to real-time, not the absence of Kafka.
The forward path is incremental or push-based PageRank over a persistent graph, plus the pattern
you already invented for Louvain — compute once, freeze, publish a fingerprint, refresh on a
schedule — generalised to the other expensive features. Say that. It demonstrates you know where
the bottleneck is, which is the actual signal of production thinking; bolting on a streaming layer
in front of a per-request full recompute demonstrates the opposite.

**One thing to fix in the demo narrative before you present.** **Measured**: `serving_context_edges.csv`
is edge-for-edge identical to `val_edges.csv` (60,379 rows, same multiset, 2025-03-02 → 2025-04-30),

is spent once", volunteer this before you are asked — either serve the context from the train
